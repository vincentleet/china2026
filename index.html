Food — authentic & local
🥣
Local canteen breakfast — jianbing & douzhir

Beijing's street breakfast culture is extraordinary and almost entirely missed by tourists. Jianbing (an egg and chilli crêpe cooked on a griddle in front of you) is the iconic one. The real foodie test is douzhir — a fermented mung bean milk that Beijingers drink with fried dough rings. Sharply divisive but worth trying. Find it at a neighbourhood canteen before 9am. A genuinely authentic food experience that costs almost nothing.

🍖
Yangrou paomo — local breakfast ritual

Xi'an's most iconic local breakfast — a rich mutton broth into which you tear and crumble a dense flatbread by hand (the tearing is done by you, and the size matters). Food & cooking
🥟
The xiaolongbao trail

A proper foodie activity — eating xiaolongbao at three or four different establishments across your Shanghai days and comparing the differences in skin thickness, broth intensity, and filling quality. Din Tai Fung is the famous benchmark but locals debate whether Jia Jia Tang Bao or Fu Chun does it better. Yang's Dumplings for the pan-fried sheng jian bao version is an equally essential comparison. Cheap enough to do multiple times without thinking about it.

Longtang neighbourhood — authentic local Shanghai

The old lane house neighbourhoods (longtang) away from the French Concession tourist strip — elderly residents playing mahjong on the pavement, laundry strung between buildings, local wet market vendors, hole-in-the-wall noodle shops with three tables and one dish. Jing'an and Putuo districts have well-preserved longtang. The side of Shanghai most visitors never find because it doesn't have a sign. Just walk in and wander.
